# Chapter 4: The House at the End of Maple Street

<!-- ilustracion: Golden afternoon on the back steps of Mari's house: Mari sitting with her chin in her hands, Zib floating beside her with a curious tilt; Pepper perched far away on top of the wooden fence staring at Zib with huge round eyes; pumpkins in the garden. -->

"What is a witch?" asked Zib.

Mari and Zib were sitting on the back steps. Pepper was sitting far away, on top of the fence, watching Zib with big round eyes.

"A witch is… well, it's a pretend thing," said Mari. "In stories, a witch has a pointy hat and a broom, and she flies on the broom, and she makes potions in a big pot. Sometimes witches in stories are mean."

"And there is one in the house at the end of the street?"

Mari wiggled her boots. "That's what the kids say. Her name is Mrs. Crump. She lives all by herself. She never comes to the block party. She has a big hat, and she walks around her yard at night, and there's a weird metal tube on her roof."

"Have you talked to her?" asked Zib.

Mari stopped wiggling. "Well… no."

---

Zib floated up in front of Mari's face. "So you do not know if she is mean."

"No," said Mari. "But the kids say—"

"On my planet," said Zib, "we have a word. *Blorp.* It means a thing you are scared of, because you do not know it yet. Most blorps are not scary. They are just new."

Mari thought about that. She thought about how scared Pepper was of Zib. Pepper thought Zib was a blorp. But Zib was the nicest, silliest blorp in the whole world.

"Maybe Mrs. Crump is a blorp," said Mari.

"Maybe," said Zib. "Let's find out?"

Mari smiled. That was her line. "Let's find out."

---

But first, Mari asked Abuela. Mari was not allowed to go to a house by herself, and she did not want to.

"Mrs. Crump?" said Abuela. "I have wanted to meet her for a long time. I see her light on very late at night. Let's bring her some pan dulce."

So Abuela put six sweet breads in a basket and covered them with a pretty cloth. Mari put Zib in her backpack.

"Stay in there," Mari whispered.

"I am a very good stayer," said Zib.

"You stayed for about one minute today."

"That is very good for me," said Zib.

---

<!-- ilustracion: Late afternoon on Maple Street: Mari with her backpack (three glowing antennae sticking out) holding Abuela's hand as they walk up to an old tall house covered in red vines, with stone garden statues, tall grasses, and a big brass telescope dome on the roof; the house looks old but the windows glow warm. -->

They walked down Maple Street. The sun was going down, and the sky was turning orange and pink.

The houses on Maple Street all had Halloween decorations. The yellow house had a scarecrow. The blue house had a ghost made from a sheet. The house with the big plastic spider had a sign that said "BEWARE OF THE SPIDER." The spider had a party hat on.

Then they came to the very last house.

Up close, the house was old and tall. Thick red vines climbed all over the walls. The yard had tall, waving grasses and stone statues of rabbits and owls. On the roof, there was a round dome, like a little metal hat, with a long brass tube poking out.

Mari held Abuela's hand a little tighter.

---

Abuela knocked on the door. *Knock, knock, knock.*

For a long time, nothing happened.

Then they heard footsteps. Slow, heavy footsteps. *Clomp. Clomp. Clomp.*

The door creaked open.

A tall woman stood there. She had silver hair in a long braid. She had a very big, very floppy black hat. She had little round glasses, and she was frowning.

"Yes?" she said, in a deep voice.

Mari wanted to hide behind Abuela. Maybe in a rain boot, like Pepper.

But Abuela just smiled her big smile. "Good evening! I am Lupe Ríos, from the white house with the pumpkins. And this is my granddaughter, Mari. We brought you some pan dulce."

---

The woman looked at the basket. She looked at Abuela. She looked at Mari.

Then the frown went away. In its place came a smile so big that her glasses went up on her cheeks.

"Pan dulce!" said Mrs. Crump. "Oh, how kind. I'm so sorry about my grumpy face. I was up all night, and I'm only half awake. I'm Hazel Crump. Please, come in!"

Mari blinked. Mrs. Crump did not sound like a witch at all. She sounded like a librarian.

"Why were you up all night?" asked Mari.

Mrs. Crump's eyes twinkled behind her glasses. "Because, my dear, that's when the stars come out."

---

<!-- ilustracion: Inside a cozy round attic room with a domed ceiling open to the evening sky; a big brass telescope in the middle; star maps and moon pictures pinned on the walls; Mrs. Crump in her floppy black hat and round glasses showing Mari a star chart; Zib peeking out of the backpack on Mari's back with wide eyes. -->

Mrs. Crump's house was not scary inside. It was warm and messy and wonderful.

There were books everywhere. Books on the shelves, books on the chairs, books on the stairs. There were maps of the stars on the walls. There were pictures of the moon, big and gray and bumpy, with little circles all over it.

"Those circles are craters," said Mrs. Crump. "Holes, where space rocks hit the moon a long, long time ago."

---

Mrs. Crump took them up the stairs to a round room at the very top of the house. The roof of this room could open up, like a window to the sky. In the middle stood a huge brass telescope.

"That's the tube on your roof!" said Mari.

"Yes," said Mrs. Crump. "I was a star scientist for forty years. An astronomer. I still look at the sky every night. I'm not a very good sleeper, but I'm a very good looker."

---

"A telescope works like a giant eye," said Mrs. Crump. "It catches lots and lots of light from far away. Then it makes the light small and bright, so it fits in your eye. That's how we can see things that are very far away."

Abuela was looking at a picture of Saturn, with its big beautiful rings.

Mrs. Crump leaned down close to Mari. She spoke very quietly.

"Mari," she said, "last night I saw something very strange in my telescope. A green light. It came down from the sky, and I think it landed near your house."

Mari's heart went *bump, bump, bump*.

Then the backpack went *zzzzip*.

---

Zib floated out of the backpack. It bobbed in the air, right in front of Mrs. Crump's round glasses.

"Hello-treat," said Zib.

Mrs. Crump did not scream. She did not faint. She took off her glasses. She cleaned them on her sweater. She put them back on.

"Well," she said softly. "Forty years of looking at the sky. And the sky came to look at me."

Mari looked over at Abuela. But Abuela was across the room, with her back to them. She was reading a book about the moon, and humming a song.

"Please," whispered Mari. "It's a secret. Zib needs to get home. Can you keep a secret?"

Mrs. Crump smiled. "My dear, I'm a scientist. I'm very good at being quiet and watching. Your secret is safe with me."

---

<!-- ilustracion: Twilight in Mrs. Crump's yard: a stone bird bath shaped like a big flower, with a lime-green glowing seed sitting in the water; Mari reaching for it while Mrs. Crump holds a lantern; Zib's antennae glowing yellow; a stone owl statue nearby. -->

Zib's antennae lit up yellow. They pointed out the window, down into the yard.

"The glow-seed," said Zib. "It is down there."

They all went down to the yard, Abuela too. "Let's look at your garden before it gets too dark," said Mrs. Crump. She carried a lantern. Zib hid behind Mari's back.

In the middle of the yard stood a stone bird bath, shaped like a big flower. The water inside was glowing green.

Mari looked in. There, at the bottom of the water, was a round, shiny glow-seed.

"Two!" whispered Mari, and she picked it up. It was warm and tingly.

"How pretty," said Abuela, who could not see Zib. "Is it a marble?"

"Something like that," said Mrs. Crump, and winked at Mari.

---

When it was time to go, Mrs. Crump walked them to the gate. The sky was dark now. The first stars were out.

"Mrs. Crump," said Mari, "everybody on our street thinks you're a witch."

Mrs. Crump laughed a big laugh. "Well, I do love a big hat. And I'm up at night. And I'm a bit grumpy before my coffee. I suppose I can see why."

"You're not a witch," said Mari. "You're a blorp."

"A what?"

"Something you're scared of only because it's new," said Mari. "I'll explain later."

Mrs. Crump leaned down. "I'll tell you something, Mari. Last night, I didn't see just one green light. I saw a second, smaller one, falling far away. Out past town. Out where the corn grows tall."

Mari and Zib looked at each other.

"The farm," said Mari. "Harvest Hill Farm. It has a giant corn maze."
