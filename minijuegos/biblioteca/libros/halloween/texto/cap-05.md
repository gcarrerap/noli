# Chapter 5: Lost in the Corn Maze

<!-- ilustracion: Sunny Saturday morning at Harvest Hill Farm: a red barn, hay bales, a big pile of pumpkins and a tractor pulling a wagon full of hay with families on it; Mari in her yellow hoodie riding on the wagon with her backpack on her lap, Zib's three antennae peeking out of it; Mom and Dad beside her. -->

On Saturday, the whole family went to Harvest Hill Farm.

Mom drove. Dad sang songs. Abuela brought a big bag of oranges. Mari brought her backpack, and her backpack brought Zib.

"Is this a farm?" Zib whispered from inside. "On my planet, we grow food in tall glass towers. With music."

"Here it grows in dirt," Mari whispered back. "With cows."

"What is a cow?"

"You'll see."

The farm was big and busy. There was a red barn and a pile of pumpkins as tall as a car. There was a wagon full of hay, pulled by a green tractor. Kids sat on the hay and bounced and laughed.

And at the end of the farm, there was a field of corn taller than Dad.

---

That was the corn maze.

A sign at the start had a big map. The paths in the corn twisted and turned like spaghetti.

"This maze is shaped like a giant bat," said Dad. "From an airplane, you can see its wings."

Mari and Dad went in together. Zib floated low, between them, close to the ground, where the corn leaves hid it.

The corn went *shhh, shhh* in the wind. Everything looked the same. Corn on the left. Corn on the right. Corn, corn, corn.

At the first corner, they turned right. At the next corner, they turned left. Then right again.

Then they came to a dead end.

---

<!-- ilustracion: Inside the tall corn maze, golden corn stalks on both sides and a blue sky above; Mari walking with her right hand touching the corn wall, looking determined; Dad behind her smiling; Zib floating low near the ground with its antennae glowing yellow and pointing ahead. -->

"Hmm," said Dad. "I think we are lost."

Zib's antennae turned yellow. They wiggled one way, then the other.

"The glow-seed is close-ish," said Zib quietly. "But the corn is in the way."

Then Mari remembered something. Ms. Patel had taught them a maze trick.

"Dad," she said, "if you put one hand on the wall and never let go, you will get out of almost any maze."

"Really?" said Dad.

"Really. It might take a long time. But you won't get lost forever."

So Mari put her right hand on the corn. She kept it there. She walked, and walked, and turned every time the wall turned.

---

The trick worked. They passed a little bench. They passed a sign that said "BAT WING." They passed the same yellow flower two times. Mari did not let go.

Zib's antennae got brighter. Yellow, more yellow, VERY yellow.

At the next turn, there was a scarecrow.

It sat on a hay bale. It had a straw hat, a plaid shirt and a big, silly pumpkin for a head. In its lap was a basket of apples.

Zib zoomed up to it. "Hello-treat, scarecrow!"

And the scarecrow said, "HELLO!"

Dad jumped. Mari jumped. Zib flipped upside down.

Then the scarecrow started to laugh.

---

<!-- ilustracion: A friendly farmer dressed as a scarecrow sitting on a hay bale in the corn maze, laughing, lifting his silly pumpkin mask to show a kind bearded face; a basket of apples in his lap with one marble-sized lime-green glow-seed shining among the red apples; Mari laughing with her hand on her heart; Zib upside down in the air. -->

The scarecrow took off its pumpkin head. It was not a scarecrow at all. It was a man with a gray beard and a big smile.

"Sorry, sorry!" he said. "I'm Farmer Joe. I sit here every Saturday to surprise people. It's my favorite part of the whole year."

Mari laughed so hard she had to sit on the hay. Dad laughed too.

"You got me," said Dad.

Farmer Joe looked at Zib. "And what a great alien costume, young lady. Is it a balloon?"

"Something like that," said Mari.

Farmer Joe held out his basket. "Take an apple for the road."

Mari looked in the basket. Red apple. Red apple. Red apple. And one round, bright, lime-green thing that was not an apple at all.

---

"Can I have that one?" asked Mari.

"That funny green one?" said Farmer Joe. "I found it in the corn on Thursday. It glows at night! I thought it was some kind of toy."

"It's a very special toy," said Mari.

"Then it's yours," said Farmer Joe.

Mari held the glow-seed in her hand. It was warm and tingly. "Three," she whispered to Zib.

"Three," Zib whispered back, and its antennae turned pink.

Then Mari put her right hand back on the corn wall, and they walked, and walked, and turned. And at last, they came out of the bat's left wing, right next to the barn, where Mom and Abuela were waiting with apple cider.

---

That night, Mari put the third glow-seed into the jar. *Click!* Three buttons were green now. The pumpkin-ship hummed louder.

But then the hum changed.

It went up and down, slow and soft, like a song. It sounded like the kind of song you sing when you miss somebody.

Zib floated very still beside the ship. Its antennae went gray.

"What is that?" asked Mari.

"That is my ship," said Zib. "It is singing a home song. It sings it when it is far from home for too long." Zib was quiet for a moment. "I think it misses home. Like me."

Mari put her hand on the warm pumpkin. She listened to the sad, soft song.

And from the kitchen window, a light came on.
