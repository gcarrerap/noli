# Chapter 7: Reading Night at the Library

On Monday night, the town library had a special party. It was called Spooky Story Night.

Kids came in pajamas and costumes. There were paper pumpkins on the shelves and orange lights around the windows. The librarian, Mr. Gómez, wore a cape and a top hat. There was hot cocoa in little paper cups.

Mari came in her astronaut helmet. Abuela came with her, holding her hand.

And Zib came in the backpack, as usual.

"Zib," Mari whispered at the door, "a library is a quiet place. You have to whisper."

"I am the best whisperer," Zib whispered, very loudly.

Abuela coughed to hide a laugh.

Mr. Gómez read a story about a ghost who was scared of people. Everybody laughed when the ghost hid in a laundry basket.

Then it was time to find books. Everybody could take one book home.

Mari went to the space books. That was her favorite shelf. There were books about rockets, and astronauts, and the sun, and the moon.

Zib peeked out of the backpack. Its eyes went big and sparkly.

"So many books about the sky!" it whispered.

Mari found a big book about planets. She opened it on a table in the corner, where nobody could see. Zib floated out and sat on the page.

---

The book showed all the planets that go around our sun.

"This is Mercury," Mari read. "It is the closest to the sun. This is Venus, and this is Earth. That's us! This is Mars, the red one."

"Red," said Zib. "Like the boots of Mari."

They turned the pages. Jupiter was big and stripy. Saturn had wide, beautiful rings. Uranus and Neptune were blue and very far away.

Zib looked at every page. It looked very carefully. Then its antennae drooped.

"My planet is not here," said Zib. "It is purple, with green rings. I do not see it."

Mari thought hard. Then she remembered something Mrs. Crump had said.

"Zib, these are only the planets that go around *our* sun," she said. "Mrs. Crump told me the stars are suns too. Far, far away. And lots of those suns have their own planets."

Zib looked up. "So my planet goes around a different star?"

"I think so," said Mari. "A star so far away, it looks tiny from here."

Zib floated to the window. It looked out at the night sky, at all the little stars.

"One of those is my sun," it said softly. "Somewhere." Then its antennae turned pink again. "That is a nice thing to know."

---

Then the antennae changed again. They flickered. Pink. Then yellow. Then very yellow.

Zib spun around in the air. The antennae pointed back at the space shelf. Not at the planet books. At the very bottom shelf, at a thin blue book.

The book was called *All About the Moon.*

Mari pulled it out. It was heavier than it should be. When she opened it, the pages fell open right in the middle.

And there, between the pages, pressed flat like a flower, was a glow-seed.

It lit up the whole page. On the page was a picture of the moon in all its shapes: thin, half, round.

---

"Five!" whispered Mari.

"Five!" whispered Zib, very loudly. A girl dressed as a cat looked over at them. Mari smiled and waved. The girl waved back and went on reading.

"How did it get in here?" Mari wondered.

"The night I land, the seeds fly very far," said Zib. "Maybe one flies in the library window. Maybe it falls in the book. Maybe a person puts the book back on the shelf without looking."

Mari put the glow-seed in her pocket. Then she checked out *All About the Moon.* She wanted to read it, too.

---

At home, Mari put the fifth glow-seed in the jar. *Click!*

The little screen on the side of the jar showed a full moon and the number four.

"Four more days," said Mari. "And three more seeds."

Zib was already floating up, its antennae standing tall and yellow. They pointed out over the fence, down the street, toward Willow Creek Elementary again.

"Back to my school?" said Mari. "But we already found the seed in my classroom."

"Not your classroom," said Zib. "The big room. With the high ceiling and the loud floor."

"The gym!" said Mari.

Then she remembered. That week, the big kids were building something in the gym for the Halloween fair.

Something with cardboard walls and fake cobwebs, and a sign on the door that said, in big drippy letters, HAUNTED HOUSE.
