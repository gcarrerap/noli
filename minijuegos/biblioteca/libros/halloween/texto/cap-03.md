# Chapter 3: The Best Costume Ever

Zib loved the backpack.

"It is like a little house," said Zib from inside. "A little house with a zipper door. And there is a banana in here! Hello-treat, banana!"

"Please don't eat my banana," said Mari.

"I only say hello to it," said Zib. "Then I eat it."

Mari walked to school with Mom. The backpack was a little bit heavy, and once in a while it hummed. Mom did not notice. Mom was talking about the costume parade.

"I can't believe it's Costume Day already," said Mom. "Do you have your helmet?"

"Right here," said Mari. She held up the silver box. The backpack wiggled.

---

At Willow Creek Elementary, every class was ready for the Fall Costume Parade. After lunch, all the kids would walk around the playground in their costumes. All the families would come to watch and clap.

Mari's class was very excited. Mari's teacher, Ms. Patel, had a big pumpkin on her desk. She said the pumpkin would get a prize ribbon for the best costume, after the parade.

Mari hung her backpack on her hook. She whispered into it, "Stay in there. Please, please stay in there."

"I stay," Zib whispered back. "I am very good at staying."

The backpack's three antennae poked out of the top, glowing yellow.

Mari pushed them back in. They popped right out again.

---

All morning, Mari tried to do her work. Ms. Patel was teaching about leaves. In the fall, she said, trees stop making the green stuff that they use to eat sunlight. When the green goes away, you can see the yellow and orange that were hiding there all along.

"So the leaves aren't turning orange," said Mari's friend Leo. "They were orange the whole time?"

"That's right," said Ms. Patel. "The green was just on top."

Mari thought that was very cool. She wanted to tell Zib.

But when she looked at her hook, her backpack was open.

And her backpack was empty.

---

<!-- ilustracion: Back of a bright classroom: an open costume box overflowing with capes, masks and hats; Zib sitting on top wearing a too-big pirate hat, giggling, antennae poking through the hat; Mari in the foreground turning around with wide surprised eyes. -->

Mari's heart jumped. *Bump, bump, bump.*

She looked under her desk. She looked in the reading corner. She looked behind the big plant by the window.

No Zib.

Then she heard a giggle. A high, bubbly giggle, like a bird and a kazoo.

It came from the back of the room, where the class kept the costume box. The box was full of old hats and capes and masks for dress-up time.

On top of the box sat a pirate hat. Under the pirate hat was a purple face with two big sparkly eyes.

"Zib!" Mari whispered.

"Mari! Look! I am a pirate-friend!" said Zib. "Arrrr-treat!"

---

Mari ran over and picked up Zib. She was just about to put it back in the backpack, when the bell rang.

"Line up, everybody!" said Ms. Patel. "It's time for the parade!"

All the kids got in a line. Mari was stuck. She had her helmet in one hand. She had Zib in the other hand.

"Oh, Mari!" said Ms. Patel. "What a wonderful costume! An astronaut. And you made a little alien friend too!"

Mari did not know what to say. So she said, "Um. Yes."

"Is it a puppet?" asked Leo.

"Sort of," said Mari.

Zib's antennae turned pink. It waved at Leo.

"Whoa," said Leo. "It's so real."

---

<!-- ilustracion: The school playground costume parade under a blue sky: kids dressed as a dinosaur, a fairy, a superhero-like hero in a homemade cape and a pirate; Mari in her silver helmet walking proudly with Zib floating on her shoulder; parents clapping by the fence. -->

The parade was so much fun.

There was a dinosaur and a fairy. There was a hot dog and a robot and two pirates. There was a girl dressed as a bunch of grapes, with purple balloons all over her shirt.

"Look," Zib whispered. "Another one of me!"

"That's a bunch of grapes," whispered Mari.

The families clapped and cheered. Zib loved the cheering. It floated a little higher off Mari's shoulder. It waved its tiny hands. Its antennae flashed every color, one after the other, like a light show.

"How does she make it float?" one dad said.

"I don't know," said a mom. "It must be a magnet. Or a drone."

Mari smiled and kept walking. Her helmet wobbled. Her heart was going fast, but it was a happy kind of fast now.

---

After the parade, everybody went back inside. Ms. Patel stood next to the big pumpkin on her desk. She held up a shiny blue ribbon.

"Everyone's costume was fantastic," said Ms. Patel. "But the ribbon for the Most Creative Costume goes to… Mari and her alien friend!"

The class clapped. Leo clapped the loudest.

Mari walked to the front. Zib floated with her.

But when Zib got close to the big pumpkin, its antennae stopped flashing. They turned bright yellow. Brighter than ever. All three of them pointed right at the pumpkin on Ms. Patel's desk.

"Mari," Zib whispered. "It is in there. A glow-seed is in there."

---

<!-- ilustracion: Close-up at Ms. Patel's desk: the top of the class pumpkin lifted off, and a small round seed glowing bright lime-green inside among the regular pumpkin seeds; Mari holding a blue ribbon and reaching in; Zib's antennae glowing bright yellow; Ms. Patel smiling in the background. -->

Mari looked at the pumpkin. The top had been cut off and put back on, like a little hat. Ms. Patel was going to carve a face on it later.

"Ms. Patel," said Mari, "can I look inside the pumpkin? For… science?"

Ms. Patel laughed. "For science? Sure. It's full of goopy seeds."

Mari lifted the top. Inside was the goop. Inside were the seeds, flat and white and slimy.

And there, right in the middle, was one seed that was not flat, or white, or slimy.

It was round, like a marble. And it was glowing. A bright, warm, lime-green glow.

Mari reached in and picked it up. It felt warm, and a little bit tingly, like the feeling when your foot is waking up.

"Huh," said Ms. Patel. "That's a funny seed."

"It's a very special seed," said Mari.

---

That afternoon, after school, Mari ran to the garden. Zib floated next to her as fast as it could go.

Mari put the glowing seed into the glass jar inside the pumpkin-ship. It fit into the first hole perfectly. *Click!*

The ship hummed a little louder. One of the round buttons turned green.

"One!" said Zib. It did a happy flip in the air. "One glow-seed! Seven more to go!"

"One down," said Mari. "Seven to go."

She hung the blue ribbon on the pumpkin's silver stem. It looked very nice there.

---

Then Zib went still again. Its antennae stood up, straight and tall. The little lights turned yellow, and they pointed.

They pointed down the street. Past the yellow house. Past the blue house. Past the house with the big plastic spider on the roof.

They pointed all the way to the end of Maple Street. To an old, tall house, with dark windows and twisty vines all over the walls. There was a strange metal thing on the roof, like a giant tube. The yard was full of tall weeds and stone statues.

Mari's tummy flipped over.

Every kid on Maple Street knew about that house. Every kid on Maple Street said the same thing about it.

"Zib," said Mari in a very small voice, "that's the house where the witch lives."
