# Chapter 10: Trick or Treat!

At last, it was Halloween.

Mari put on her white hoodie with the stars and planets. She put on her shiny silver helmet. It still wobbled a little, but that was okay. It was perfect.

Leo came over in his knight costume, with his cardboard shield. Pepper had tiny purple bat wings on her back, which Mom had made out of felt. Pepper did not love the wings. But she did not take them off, either.

And Zib?

"What is your costume, Zib?" asked Leo.

Zib floated up proudly. "I am dressed as Zib!"

Abuela laughed and gave Zib a little orange bucket. "Then you are the best-dressed Zib in town."

Dad came too, with a flashlight. "Stay together," he said. "Walk, don't run. And say thank you."

"We will," said Mari.

The sun was going down. The sky was pink and purple. And over the trees, the moon was rising. It was big and round and orange, like a giant pumpkin in the sky.

"The full moon," whispered Zib. "It is here."

"Zib," whispered Mari, "the seeds?"

Zib's antennae were bright yellow. "Two," it said. "Close. Very close-ish."

Down Maple Street, all the porch lights were on. Kids in costumes were everywhere. There were witches and pirates and dinosaurs and one very tall banana.

---

At the first house, Mari knocked on the door.

A lady opened it. She held a bowl of candy.

"Trick or treat!" said Mari and Leo.

"Hello-treat!" said Zib.

The lady laughed and put a candy in each bucket, even Zib's. "What an amazing alien," she said.

Zib looked at the candy in its bucket. Then it looked at Mari.

"Mari," it said, "I understand now. Trick-or-treat is not a hello. It is a… a door game. You knock, and people are kind to you, and you say thank you."

"That's right," said Mari.

Zib turned back to the lady. "Thank you very much," it said, and bowed upside down.

---

They went from house to house. The yellow house had a scarecrow. The blue house had a sheet ghost. At every house, Zib said, "Hello-treat! Thank you very much!"

But the glow-seeds were hard to find. The antennae kept wiggling. First they pointed left. Then right. Then behind them.

"They keep moving," said Zib.

Then Leo pointed down the sidewalk. A little boy, about three years old, was walking with his dad. He was dressed as a pumpkin. And in his hand, he was holding something round and glowing green.

"Is that it?" said Leo.

Zib's antennae went so bright yellow they almost looked white.

Mari walked over and knelt down.

"Hi," she said to the little boy. "I love your costume. That's a cool glowing ball."

"Mine," said the little boy. "I found it. In my yard."

Mari thought about it. She looked in her bucket. She had a big chocolate bar. The biggest one she had gotten all night.

"Do you want to trade?" she asked. "This for that?"

The little boy looked at the chocolate. He looked at the glowing ball. He looked at the chocolate again.

"Okay!" he said, and they traded.

"Thank you," Mari said. "This is really important."

His dad smiled. "That's very kind of you," he said. "He's been carrying it around all week."

"Seven," whispered Leo.

---

There was only one seed left.

Zib's antennae pointed to the house with the big plastic spider on the roof. The spider still had its party hat on.

The man who lived there, Mr. Lee, was sitting on the porch. Next to him was a giant bowl of candy. And on top of the candy, right in the middle, sat a round green light.

"I found this in my gutter on Monday," said Mr. Lee. "It glows! I thought it made a nice decoration for my candy bowl."

"Mr. Lee," said Mari, "could I please have the green one, instead of candy?"

Mr. Lee laughed. "Instead of candy? That's a first. Sure, you can have it." He gave her the glow-seed. Then he gave her two candies anyway.

"Eight!" said Mari, and Leo, and Zib, all at the same time.

They ran home. Walked, actually, because Dad said walk. But they walked very, very fast.

In the garden, Mari opened the pumpkin-ship. She put the seventh seed in the jar. *Click!* She put the eighth seed in the jar. *Click!*

All eight buttons turned green.

For a second, nothing happened. Then the pumpkin-ship started to glow. First a little. Then more. Then so much that the whole garden turned green, like they were standing under the sea.

Dad's mouth fell open. He looked at Zib. He looked at Mari.

"Mari," said Dad slowly, "is that a real spaceship?"

"Yes," said Mari. "And it's almost time for Zib to go home."

Above them, the full moon was high and bright. And from the moon, all the way down to the pumpkin-ship, a thin silver line of light began to shine, like a road.
