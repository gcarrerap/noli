# Chapter 12: See You Next Halloween

In Zib's tiny hand was a seed.

It was not a glow-seed. It was smaller, and flatter, and shaped like a little teardrop. It looked almost like a pumpkin seed. But it was purple, and it had a soft, tiny glow, like a night-light.

"This is a friend-seed," said Zib. "From my planet. On my planet, when you make a very good friend, you give them a friend-seed. Then, wherever they are, they have a little piece of you."

Mari held out her hand. Zib put the friend-seed in it. It was warm, like a hug.

"Plant it in the spring," said Zib. "In Abuela's garden. With the pumpkins."

"I will," said Mari. Her voice came out wobbly. "I promise."

Then Zib floated to everybody, one by one.

It hugged Abuela around the neck. "Thank you for the candied pumpkin, big Abuela. And for the story about the little star."

"Que te vaya bonito, little star," said Abuela, and she wiped her eyes with her rebozo.

It bowed upside down to Mrs. Crump. "Thank you, star-lady. For finding my sun."

"Thank you," said Mrs. Crump, "for coming to visit an old astronomer."

It gave Leo a high five on his shield. "Thank you, knight-friend. For keeping the secret."

"Always," said Leo.

It floated down to Pepper. Pepper did not run away. She did not hide. She just reached up and touched her nose to Zib's nose.

"Thank you, small dark tiger," said Zib.

---

Last of all, Zib floated back to Mari.

For a moment, neither of them said anything.

"Mari," said Zib, "on my planet, they will ask me, 'Zib, what is Earth like?' Do you know what I will say?"

Mari shook her head. Her helmet wobbled.

"I will say, Earth is a place where you fall down in the dark, and you are lost, and you are scared," said Zib. "And then a girl in a box helmet comes and says, 'Let's find out.' And everybody helps."

Mari laughed, and cried a little, both at the same time.

"Will I ever see you again?" she asked.

Zib's antennae glowed pink and gold. "Every Halloween," it said, "the moon is round. And the moon road comes back."

Zib floated into the pumpkin-ship. The lid came down. *Click.*

The ship rose. Up past the fence. Up past the big oak tree. Up, up, up, along the silver road of moonlight.

From the little round window, a tiny purple hand waved and waved.

Everybody waved back. Mari waved with both hands. Dad held up the flashlight and blinked it on and off, like a goodbye.

Higher and higher went the pumpkin-ship. It got smaller, and smaller, until it looked like a little golden star.

Then, right in front of the moon, it stopped for one second.

It drew a long, bright green line across the sky. The line curved up at both ends.

It was a smile.

Then the little star went *zip* and was gone.

---

For a long time, everybody stood in the garden, looking up.

"Well," said Dad at last. "That was the best Halloween ever."

"The very best," said Mom.

Abuela poured everybody a cup of hot cocoa. Mrs. Crump told Leo's mom all about telescopes. Leo told everybody, again, about the mop. Pepper sat on Mari's feet and purred.

Mari held the friend-seed in her hand. It glowed softly, like a tiny piece of the moon road.

She thought about Zib, flying past the moon, past the planets, past all the stars, toward one small orange sun. Toward a mama making soup, and a papa telling jokes, and a little brother named Zub, jumping in puddles.

"Get home safe, Zib," she whispered.

In the spring, Mari planted the friend-seed in Abuela's garden, right next to the pumpkins.

All summer long, she watered it. A little vine grew. It had leaves shaped like stars.

And the next October, in the middle of Abuela's pumpkin patch, there was a small round pumpkin. It was not orange. It was purple, with soft green stripes. And at night, it glowed.

Mari was eight now. She was working on a new Halloween costume. It had a purple hat. It had three springy antennae, made from pipe cleaners and ping-pong balls.

"What are you going to be this year, mija?" asked Abuela.

Mari put on the hat. She looked up at the sky, where the moon was getting rounder every night.

"I'm going to be dressed as Zib," said Mari. "So that when Zib comes back, it will know exactly where to find me."

THE END
