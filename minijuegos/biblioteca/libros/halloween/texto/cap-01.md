# Chapter 1: Something Fell in the Pumpkin Patch

<!-- ilustracion: Mari at the kitchen table with tape, scissors and a cardboard box, building her astronaut helmet; Pepper the cat watching from a chair; Halloween decorations on the window. -->

Mari Ríos had a big box, a roll of silver tape, and a very big idea.

"I am going to be an astronaut for Halloween," she said. "A real one. With a real helmet."

She cut a round hole in the box. She cut two small holes for air. She put silver tape on every side until the box was shiny.

Pepper sat on a chair and watched. Pepper was a small black cat with big yellow eyes. Pepper did not like boxes. Pepper did not like tape. Pepper did not like the sound the scissors made.

*Snip!* went the scissors.

Pepper jumped off the chair and hid under the table.

"It's okay, Pepper," Mari said. "It is just a helmet."

---

Mari put the box on her head. She looked in the mirror by the door.

The helmet was a little bit too big. It wobbled when she walked. One side was more silver than the other side.

But it was still the best helmet Mari had ever seen.

"Houston, we have a cat," Mari said in her best astronaut voice.

Pepper peeked out from under the table. She did not look happy.

Halloween was only one week away. The whole house was ready. There were paper bats on the windows. There were orange lights on the porch. There was a big bowl for candy by the door, and it was empty, but not for long.

---

Mari's grandmother lived with Mari and her family. Mari called her Abuela.

Abuela had a garden behind the house. In the summer, the garden was full of green vines. Now, in October, the vines were brown, and the garden was full of pumpkins.

Some pumpkins were small, like a ball. Some pumpkins were big, like a chair. One pumpkin was so big that Mari could sit on it.

"Your helmet is beautiful, mija," Abuela said. She was in the kitchen, making tea. "Do you know what real astronauts look at?"

"Space?" said Mari.

"The stars," said Abuela. "Come. It is a clear night. Let's go look."

---

<!-- ilustracion: Night in the backyard pumpkin patch; Abuela in a warm shawl and Mari in her cardboard helmet sitting on the porch steps looking up at a sky full of stars and a big round moon; porch light glowing warm. -->

Mari and Abuela sat on the back steps. The air was cool. It smelled like leaves and like Abuela's cinnamon tea.

The sky was dark blue, and it was full of stars.

"When I was a little girl in Mexico," Abuela said, "my abuela told me that every star has a name. Some stars are big and hot, like our sun. Some are so far away that their light takes years and years to get here."

"Years?" said Mari. "Light is slow?"

"No, light is very fast," said Abuela. "The fastest thing there is. But space is very, very big."

Mari looked up. She tried to think about something so big. It made her head feel funny, in a good way.

---

The moon was up over the trees. It was almost round, like a cookie with a little bite gone.

"Look at the moon," said Abuela. "Every night, it gets a little rounder. On Halloween night, it will be a full moon. All round and bright."

"A full moon on Halloween!" said Mari. "That is the best kind of Halloween."

The moon does not make its own light. Abuela said the sun shines on it, like a flashlight on a ball. The moon looks different every night because we see a different part of the sunny side.

Mari liked that. A moon with a flashlight. She wanted to tell her teacher.

---

<!-- ilustracion: Wide shot of the night sky over the backyard; a bright lime-green streak of light curving down toward the pumpkin patch; Mari pointing up in surprise, her helmet tipping; Abuela turned away pouring tea. -->

Then Mari saw something.

A light was moving across the sky. It was not white like a star. It was green, a bright, bright green, like a glow stick.

It moved fast. It made a long line, and then it curved down, down, down.

It went down past the trees. It went down past the fence.

It went down right into Abuela's pumpkin patch.

There was a soft sound, like a pillow falling on the floor.

*Fwump.*

"Abuela!" said Mari. "Did you see that?"

Abuela was looking at her tea. "See what, mija?"

"A green light! It fell in the pumpkins!"

---

Abuela looked at the garden. Mari looked at the garden. The garden looked the same as always. Brown vines. Big, quiet pumpkins. Nothing was green, and nothing was glowing.

"Maybe it was a shooting star," said Abuela. "Shooting stars are little rocks from space. They burn up very high in the sky. They almost never come down to the ground."

"Almost never?" said Mari.

Abuela smiled. "Almost never. Now, it is time for bed, little astronaut."

Mari went to bed. But she did not sleep for a long time. She kept looking out her window at the dark garden. Was something out there?

---

<!-- ilustracion: Morning in the pumpkin patch; Mari in pajamas, rain boots and her cardboard helmet kneeling with her ear against one big orange pumpkin; Pepper hiding behind a watering can with only her eyes showing. -->

In the morning, Mari put on her rain boots. She put on her helmet, just in case. Then she ran out to the garden.

Pepper came too, but slowly, and she stayed far behind.

Mari walked between the pumpkins. She looked at each one. Small pumpkin. Medium pumpkin. Big pumpkin. They all looked like pumpkins.

Then she heard it.

*Hmmmmmm.*

It was a soft, humming sound. Like a refrigerator. Like a bee that was very, very sleepy.

Mari followed the sound. It came from one big pumpkin by the fence. This pumpkin was rounder than the others, and smoother, and very, very orange.

---

Mari put her ear on the pumpkin.

*Hmmmmmm.*

She put her hand on the pumpkin. It was warm. Not hot, just warm, like a cup of cocoa that was ready to drink.

"Pepper," Mari said. "This pumpkin is humming."

Pepper hid behind the watering can. Only her yellow eyes were showing.

Mari looked closer. There was a thin line all the way around the middle of the pumpkin, like the line on a lunch box. And on the top, the stem was not brown. It was silver.

Mari's heart went fast. *Bump, bump, bump.*

---

"Hello?" Mari said to the pumpkin. "Is anybody in there?"

Nothing happened.

Mari waited. She counted to ten. She felt a little bit silly, talking to a pumpkin.

"Okay," she said. "I guess you are just a pumpkin."

She turned to go back to the house.

Then, behind her, a tiny light came on. Right on the side of the pumpkin, a small round window glowed green. It went bright, and then dark, and then bright again.

The pumpkin was blinking.

And it was blinking right at Mari.
