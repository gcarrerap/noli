# Chapter 6: Abuela Finds Out

The kitchen light was Abuela, making a late cup of tea. She looked out the window, but all she saw was the garden, dark and quiet. She yawned and went to bed.

Mari let out a big breath.

But on Sunday morning, Abuela came down to the kitchen very early. Earlier than anybody. Earlier than the sun.

And there, on the counter, was a small purple alien with its whole face in the sugar jar.

*Crunch, crunch, crunch.*

Abuela stopped in the doorway. She put on her slippers. She looked again.

The alien lifted its head out of the jar. It had sugar on its cheeks. It had sugar on its antennae.

"Hello-treat," said Zib.

Mari heard a crash. She ran down the stairs in her pajamas.

"Abuela! Wait! I can explain!"

But Abuela was not screaming. Abuela was not running. Abuela was doing something very surprising.

Abuela was laughing.

"Ay, mija," she said. "So this is why my sugar is always gone!"

Mari stopped on the last step. "You're not scared?"

Abuela pulled out a chair and sat down. "When I was a little girl, my grandmother told me that the stars sometimes send visitors. I always hoped I would meet one." She smiled at Zib. "Good morning, little star."

Zib's antennae turned pink. "Good morning, big Abuela."

---

So Mari told Abuela everything. The green light. The pumpkin-ship. The glow-seeds. The full moon on Halloween.

Abuela listened to the whole story. She did not say "That's silly." She did not say "You're making it up." She just nodded and listened.

"Eight seeds," said Abuela. "And how many have you found?"

"Three," said Mari.

"Four," said Zib.

Mari blinked. "Three, Zib. We found three."

Zib pointed one tiny finger at the sugar jar. "Four," it said again. "That is why I was in there. Not for the sugar." Zib thought about it. "Okay. A little for the sugar."

---

Abuela got a big spoon. Mari dug into the sugar. Down, down, down, to the very bottom of the jar.

There it was. Round and warm and glowing green, with sugar sticking to it like snow.

"It must have rolled in through the window," said Abuela. "The night it fell, I left the window open. I always do when I cook."

"Four!" said Mari.

Zib did three flips in a row. Sugar flew everywhere.

Pepper watched from the top of the refrigerator. She did not like flips. She did not like flying sugar. She stayed exactly where she was.

---

"Now," said Abuela, "a visitor should not eat plain sugar for breakfast. A visitor should eat something special."

She took a small pumpkin from the garden. She cut it into pieces. She put the pieces in a big pot with brown sugar, cinnamon sticks and water.

"Calabaza en tacha," said Abuela. "Candied pumpkin. My mother made it every year for Día de Muertos."

The kitchen started to smell sweet and warm, like cinnamon and caramel. Mari helped stir. Zib floated over the pot and sniffed and sniffed.

When it was ready, Abuela put one piece on a small plate for Zib.

Zib took a bite.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then Zib began to glow. Not just its antennae. All of Zib. It turned pink, then orange, then yellow, then green, then blue, like a rainbow moving from its head down to its belly.

"Oh," said Zib. "Oh, oh, oh. This is the best food in the whole universe."

Abuela laughed and gave Zib another piece.

While they ate, Abuela told them a story. It was about a little star that fell out of the sky and got lost in a big dark forest. The star was scared. But the animals of the forest helped it. The owl showed it the way, and the rabbit kept it warm. And on the clearest night of the year, the little star floated back up to its family.

"Everybody gets lost sometimes," said Abuela. "That is why we help each other find the way."

---

Zib floated over and gave Abuela a small, sticky hug around her neck.

"Thank you, big Abuela," said Zib.

"De nada, little star," said Abuela. "In this house, there is always room for one more."

After breakfast, Mari and Zib ran out to the garden. Mari put the fourth glow-seed in the jar. *Click!* Four buttons were green now.

Then something new happened. A little screen on the side of the jar lit up. It showed two glowing shapes: a full moon, and the number five.

"What does that mean?" asked Mari.

Zib's antennae drooped a little. "It means four seeds in the jar," it said. "Four more to find. And only five more days until the moon is full."
