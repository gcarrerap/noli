# Chapter 11: Countdown in the Pumpkin Patch

Soon, everybody was in the garden.

Mom came out in her slippers. Abuela came out with a pot of hot cocoa. Leo's mom came to pick him up, saw the glowing pumpkin, and stayed. And Mrs. Crump came down Maple Street as fast as she could, holding her lantern and her big floppy hat.

"I saw the light from my roof!" she said, out of breath. "Is it time?"

"It's time," said Zib.

Everybody stood in a circle around the pumpkin-ship. It hummed. It glowed. The silver road of moonlight shone down on it from the sky.

Nobody was scared. Not even Mom. Well, maybe a little bit, at first. But then Zib floated over and said, "Hello-treat, Mari's mama," and Mom laughed and said hello back.

Mrs. Crump put down her lantern. She took out a little notebook and a pencil. She looked at the moon. She looked at the ship. She wrote numbers in her notebook.

"Zib," she said, "the moon road will be strongest when the moon is highest in the sky. That will be in about ten minutes. You'll want to go straight up, and then follow the light."

"Ten minutes," said Zib. "You are a very smart star-lady."

Mrs. Crump smiled so big that her glasses went up on her cheeks.

Zib floated into the pumpkin-ship. It sat in the tiny seat. It pushed some buttons. The ship hummed louder and louder.

Then, suddenly, the ship gave a big shake.

*BUMP!*

---

The jar in the middle of the ship rattled. And one glow-seed popped right out of its hole.

It bounced off the edge of the pumpkin. It bounced on the ground. Then it rolled away, under the big brown leaves, deep into the darkest corner of the garden, way back by the fence.

"Oh no!" cried Zib. "The ship cannot go with only seven!"

Everybody started looking. Dad pointed his flashlight. Mom moved the leaves. Leo crawled on the ground. But the corner was thick with vines and old pumpkins, and the seed was hiding under all of it.

"I can't see it anywhere," said Leo.

"Eight minutes," said Mrs. Crump, looking at her watch.

Then something small and black moved through the grass.

It was Pepper.

Pepper, who was scared of boxes, and tape, and scissors. Pepper, who was scared of mops, and flips, and flying sugar. Pepper, who had hidden in a rain boot the very first time she saw Zib.

Pepper walked to the edge of the dark vines. Her purple bat wings flopped. Her ears went forward. Her yellow eyes went big.

She looked back at Mari.

"You can do it, Pepper," whispered Mari.

And Pepper went in.

---

For a few seconds, nobody could see her. The vines shook. The leaves rustled. Somebody's breath went in and did not come out.

"Six minutes," whispered Mrs. Crump.

Then the leaves moved again. And out came Pepper, walking very proudly, with her tail straight up in the air.

In her mouth, held very gently, like a mother cat carries a kitten, was a small, round, glowing glow-seed.

She walked right past everybody. She walked right up to the pumpkin-ship. And she dropped the glow-seed at Zib's feet. Well, at the place where Zib's feet would be, if Zib had feet.

"Pepper!" cried Mari. She scooped up the cat and hugged her.

"Pepper," said Zib softly. "Small dark tiger. You were very, very brave."

Pepper said, "Mrrp." That was probably cat for "I know."

Zib put the glow-seed back in the jar. *Click!* All eight buttons were green again. This time, Zib pushed the jar's lid closed, tight.

Everybody cheered. Abuela cheered the loudest. Leo held up his shield. Mrs. Crump threw her big hat in the air, and Dad had to catch it.

"Four minutes," said Mrs. Crump, a little out of breath from cheering.

---

The pumpkin-ship began to rise. It floated up, slowly, one inch above the ground. Then two. Its light turned from green to gold.

But just before the lid came down, Zib floated back out.

It floated over to Mari. It looked at her with its big, sparkly eyes. Its antennae were glowing a color Mari had never seen before. It was a soft color, somewhere between pink and gold.

"Mari," said Zib, "before I go, I have something for you."

Mari put Pepper down. She felt a lump in her throat.

"Something for me?" she said.

"Yes," said Zib, and it held out one tiny purple hand. Something inside it was glowing.
