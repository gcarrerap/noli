# Chapter 8: The Not-So-Haunted House

On Tuesday night, Willow Creek Elementary had its Halloween Fair.

There was face painting and a cake walk. There was a game where you threw rings on pumpkins. And in the gym, there was the Haunted House that the fifth graders had built.

Mari stood at the door with Mom. The sign said HAUNTED HOUSE in big, drippy, green letters. Behind the door, she could hear spooky music. *Ooooooh. Ooooooh.*

Mari's knees felt like jelly again.

"You don't have to go in," said Mom.

"I have to," said Mari. Her backpack was humming. Zib's antennae were bright, bright yellow.

---

Just then, somebody tapped Mari on the shoulder.

"Mari! Are you going in? Can I come with you?"

It was Leo, her best friend from class. He had a knight costume with a cardboard shield.

"Okay," said Mari. "Mom, can Leo and I go in together?"

"Sure," said Mom. "I'll be right here at the exit."

Mari and Leo held hands. They walked through the door.

And from inside Mom's big tote bag, where nobody had noticed her, a small black cat jumped out and followed them.

The Haunted House was dark, but not too dark. Purple lights glowed on the cardboard walls. There were paper bats hanging from strings, and cotton cobwebs in every corner.

A ghost swung down from the ceiling. *Whoooosh!*

Leo grabbed Mari's arm. Then he looked again.

"It's a bedsheet," said Leo. "With two eyes drawn on with a marker."

They kept walking. A skeleton sat in a chair. It was made of plastic, and when they walked by, it started to sing a song about bones in a silly, wobbly voice.

Mari started to giggle. Leo started to giggle too.

"This isn't scary," said Leo. "It's funny."

---

Then they heard a sound behind them.

*MRROOOW!*

Mari spun around. It was Pepper! She was standing in the middle of the path, with her fur all puffed up like a dandelion. Her tail was as big as a bottle brush.

In front of Pepper was a mop. A big gray mop with long, floppy strings, leaning on the wall.

Pepper hissed at the mop. The mop did not move.

Pepper hissed again. The mop still did not move.

Pepper jumped straight up in the air, turned around and ran right into Mari's backpack.

The backpack went *zzzzip*.

And out of the backpack, with a black cat now sitting where it used to be, floated Zib.

---

Leo stared at Zib. Zib stared at Leo.

"Whoa," said Leo.

"Hello-treat," said Zib.

"Leo," said Mari quickly, "this is Zib. Zib is real. Zib is from another planet, and Zib is my friend, and Zib needs our help, and it's a secret. A big, big secret."

Leo's mouth was still open.

"Can you keep a secret?" asked Mari.

Leo closed his mouth. He nodded. He held up his cardboard shield, like a real knight making a promise.

"I promise," he said. "On my shield."

Zib's antennae turned pink. "You are a very good human-friend too," it said.

---

Mari told Leo about the glow-seeds as fast as she could. Leo listened with his eyes wide.

"So where's the seed?" asked Leo.

Zib's antennae were bright yellow. They pointed to the very last room of the Haunted House. Thick, white fog was rolling out from under the door.

The last room had a big black pot, like a witch's cauldron, made from a trash can. The fog was coming out of a little machine hidden behind it.

The machine was going *pfff, pfff, pfff*. And every time it puffed, the fog glowed a little bit green.

---

"It's stuck in the fog machine!" said Leo.

Mari looked closer. A glow-seed was stuck right in the hole where the fog came out.

Mari did not touch the machine. That was a rule. You don't touch machines that you don't know.

Instead, she found one of the fifth graders who was helping. Her name was Jada, and she was dressed like a friendly vampire.

"Excuse me," said Mari, "I think something is stuck in your fog machine."

Jada turned off the machine and looked. "Huh! A marble!" She pulled out the glow-seed and gave it to Mari. "Thanks for telling me. It was going to break."

"Six," whispered Mari, and put it in her pocket.

---

When they came out of the Haunted House, Zib was hiding behind Leo's big cardboard shield. Mom was waiting at the exit. She was holding her empty tote bag and looking very confused.

"Mari," said Mom, "why is the cat in your backpack?"

"It's a long story," said Mari.

Leo walked them to the car. Before they said goodbye, he whispered, "Tomorrow, I want to help find the last two seeds."

Then a big, cold drop of water fell on Mari's nose. Then another. Then lots and lots of them.

It started to rain. Hard.

All the way home, Zib sat in Mari's lap, looking out the car window at the rain. It did not say "hello-treat." It did not say anything.

Its antennae had turned gray, and they did not turn any other color for the whole ride home.
